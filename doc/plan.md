# VX3 Simulator plan

## Current slice

The workstation presents a fixed six-section early-1970s VX3 rack in a low-resolution science laboratory. Each section uses community apparatus and manufacturer names where research supports them. Individual maker liveries vary between rainbow, beige, bold blocks, industrial chevrons and camouflage, all behind the hardware. The title/status overlay has been removed; camera and sound controls remain. The BBC Micro Model B sits beneath the Microvitec Cub on the calibration bench. The xenon manifold now stands above and in front of the corrected cylinder shoulders, connected by copper pigtails. The rack has switches, knobs, a band selector and pushbuttons; analogue gauges, scope and counter; one first-person perspective camera with zoom and movement controls; and an operator log. A six-position ferrocore drum and a second cabinet of amplifier, emitter and radiator gear sit to the right. A filing cabinet opens operating papers, and period advertisements hang just above the west workbench. The widened room has a proper right-hand wall carrying an idle, bracket-mounted teleprinter, with fanfold stock below and spent output piled in front; the camera can pan far enough to inspect it. A small glass ashtray rests beside the deep white porcelain sink on the west workbench. Below the flux map, a load and kWh meter and a pulsing Time Dilation crystal report network outputs. Instrument placement reserves clearance beside the rack handles. Procedural audio follows controls and rig state, including the Encabulator motor and rotation pulse. Vertical scrolling moves the camera in depth, Option/Alt plus scrolling moves it in height, and horizontal mouse swipes pan sideways.

## Retired rig

The first-generation VX3 (the 42U rack, the side cabinet and the ferrocore drum, with the wall socket and mains lead) has been taken out of the lab and kept, unwired, as an archive in room dev3 (`/?room=dev3`, `src/rendering/gen1-room.ts`), with a sign saying it was retired after dangerous faults in its turbo-encabulator. Its code is `src/scene/gen1-vx3.ts`. Its successor, the second-generation VX3 (the ferrocore column from the developer room), now stands in the bay and is wired in. The first rig's drive model is the column's ferrocore drive: worked from the column's panels and the east board, it heats the core, and it runs only in a purged, pressurised, recirculating xenon atmosphere. The east board's top row is now eighteen named alarm windows covering every warning in the installation, and the wall meter, crystal, overheat and fire, the Cub's pages and the machine's sounds follow the drive again. [The cold-start audit brief](resume/2026-10-13_gen2-cold-start-audit.md) asks the other agent to check a player can do it all, and to give the column's controls their real labels.

Every fitted input now changes the machine. RATIO gears the phase spindle and shifts the ideal trim; GND? earths the plasma inducer and slows the drift; the SERVICE-B insulation BAND sets how fast strain bleeds, fastest when it matches the field; the betaform stage gain switches stages in (lamps), scales both monitor traces and adds supply load. The [control graph](design/control-graph.md) records every link. Every rotary control owns its setting. Plasma excitation and null are separate from coil field and trim; detector, chamber and betaform gain are separate; rotary band selectors neither move each other nor overwrite drive ratio.
