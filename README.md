# VX3 Simulator

A workstation recreation of the VX3 laboratory that is still in service two floors below the Helmholtz-Bau at the University of Leipzig, Room −2.17. Its **full early-1970s VX3 cabinet** stood here until October 2026, when it was withdrawn after dangerous faults in its turbo-encabulator; its bay now holds the second-generation VX3, a ferrocore column on an olive skid between two mismatched cabinets, which is being commissioned. The apparatus around it is original; the ducting, cable trays and safety fittings are modern additions. The object of a run is to bring the heaters to temperature, establish spindle rotation, raise the field and hold a useful Delta without exceeding the rack's tolerance for drift. For educational use only.

The original equipment uses analogue regulation, valves, ferrocores and a Baltovsky chamber monitor. Its numerical counter and oscilloscope are observation instruments, not a computer control register. The later ConVX practice of driving a VX3 from a microcomputer is outside this recreation. No credible calculation of a VX8 on a conventional computer is presently available.

## Commissioning the workstation

Use Node.js 22.13.x, 24.x or 26+ and npm. From this directory:

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local address printed by the workstation, normally `http://127.0.0.1:5173/`. To cold-start the second-generation column: put the lights on at the switch by the lab door. In the adjacent TECHNIKRAUM, close the HAUPTSCHALTER beside the rectifier, open the xenon cage and turn the flow regulator until the rotameter reads at least 2 SLPM; the master supplies machinery independently of the room lights. On the east board, turn NETZ, the first handle of the KREISLAUF bay. At the column, check that the red-and-yellow isolator at the top left of the grey cabinet is ON and that the red NETZ button at the foot of that cabinet is lit. On the SÄULE station in the olive cabinet, press SENKEN and wait while the rams lower the head, about six seconds, until UNTEN burns steady; the seam puffs as it lands. Press SPANNEN; the clamps close and DICHT lights. Press XE EIN to open the xenon inlet, then open the outlet to the pump with the handwheel on the beam tube (or SPÜLEN on the east board); AUSLASS lights. Wait while the purge drives the air out, about half a minute, until the LAMBDA-SONDE needle is in the green and O2 = 0 lights. Shut the outlet, and the KAMMERDRUCK gauge climbs past the 1.2 bar working pressure towards the regulator's 1.8. Only then, on the KERN panel below, turn AKTIVIERUNG up (click its right half): past 800 K the core glows, and at 3200 K or more it runs at full power, turns blue and spins; HELLIGKEIT sets its glow. To shut down, turn AKTIVIERUNG back to the bottom, let the core cool, shut XE EIN and open NETZ on the east board. Heating the core with the head up, the clamps open or air still in the chamber burns it out; leaving the outlet open on a hot core with the xenon line shut starves it into a flux inductance catastrophe. Either way QUITT on the SÄULE station, or RÜCKS. on the east board, fits a fresh core.

The first-generation rig, kept at `/?room=dev3`, was run like this: In the adjacent TECHNIKRAUM, close the master throw switch, open the xenon cage and turn the flow regulator until the rotameter reads at least 2 SLPM. The master supplies machinery independently of the room lights. Then close the rack mains breaker, wait for the valve heaters, advance spindle speed and field excitation, then trim the local Helmholtz-Blatz regulator as the field wanders. Its “Blatz” scale and phase spindle are particular to this installation; they should not be mistaken for general VX nomenclature. A fully stable field still respects Yalgeth's 0.88 Delta limit.

Four secondary controls shape the run. RATIO sets the gearing between the Encabulator and the phase spindle and moves the trim point with it, so retrim after any change. GND? earths the plasma inducer, and the trim point then drifts far more slowly. The ion insulation BAND clears strain fastest when it matches the field strength. The betaform stage gain in the side cabinet switches amplifier stages in, one lamp each: more stages give a larger trace on both monitors and a heavier load on the supply.

Click the left half of any knob or rotary selector to turn it anticlockwise and the right half to turn it clockwise, one detent at a time; every one stops at its end stops rather than wrapping round. The emergency telephone's dial is not a knob and does not follow this rule. Knob markers rotate with their bodies about the shafts. Every knob and rotary selector has an independent setting; the plasma EXCITE and NULL controls are separate from coil FIELD and BLATZ TRIM, and each detector and amplifier has its own gain. In movement mode, aim at the appropriate half and click or press E; Escape releases the pointer. C crouches and V lowers the crouch further. Scroll to zoom in and out, up to four times closer, for working small controls; looking slows in proportion as the view narrows. A Magic Mouse swipe, the Arc Mouse touch strip and a trackpad pinch all zoom smoothly. Hold Option/Alt while scrolling to move the camera up or down, and a clearly sideways swipe steps sideways. Dragging the scene changes the viewing angle. The on-screen arrows move the operator and the plus and minus buttons zoom. The developer room at `/?room=dev` walks and zooms the same way. The operator's log records adjustments and incidents; it may be put away and reopened. Sound begins after the first operator action and may be muted or adjusted at the top left.

## Represented equipment

The first-generation rig is kept, disconnected, with a sign recording its retirement, at `/?room=dev3`, as a record of the installation the run and controls above were written for. The fixed rack includes a varistance-isolated supply, a chromocore and valve bank, an Encabulator drive, a Holtzmann resonance coil, a Fromitz detector and an ion insulation check. A six-station ferrocore drum stands beside it, followed by a smaller cabinet holding a betaform amplifier, isolated-frame flux emitter and nanocore radiator. Volt Xoccula, Neumann Dynamik, Synclex, Wonshou Labs, Bonk Industries and Gorskiy appear on the fascias. The precise maker-to-unit pairings, painted liveries, panels and wiring shown here are the recreation's own designs. Borrowed community terms and invented local parts are distinguished in the [lore register](doc/lore/summary.md) and [component catalogue](doc/lore/component-catalogue.json). How every switch, knob and lever reaches the machine, and which lamps, needles and screens report back, is drawn in the [control graph](doc/design/control-graph.md).

The surrounding laboratory includes an analogue wall clock, a map of global flux correlations, a test bench with a BBC Micro Model B beneath its Microvitec Cub monitor and a separate 5¼-inch disk drive, with an orange typist's chair pulled up to it (sit in it and the Cub is square in front of you; C stands up again), a safety notice board on the wall to the left of the desk, and the bay where the first-generation rig stood, now holding its successor. A small amber-glass ashtray sits on the west workbench beside the deep white porcelain sink, its last cigarette still burning. Below the map, a wall meter shows instantaneous load in kW and accumulated consumption in kWh, and responds to the connected apparatus in this recreation. Three period advertisements just above the west workbench offer differing accounts of VX equipment and its likely operators. Three reference drawers hold the operating guide, core-handling instructions and a redrawn signal path. A Neumann Dynamik teleprinter stands on a bracket fixed to the right-hand wall, with continuous-feed stock below and old output gathering on the floor; just above it, a prismatic Time Dilation crystal glows and sheds particles once the ferrocores are warm, powered, sufficiently supplied with xenon and settled at the selected spindle speed. The printer repeats the timestamped operator log on continuous fanfold paper, with the pin-head rasp, carriage whine and tractor-feed rattle of a dot-matrix mechanism. Its supply never visibly runs out; printed paper drops into a perpetually settling pile whose height stays roughly constant. It keeps logging while machinery power is off, on its separate supply. On the east wall stands a 1974 process board, rewired in 1986 as a remote station for the VX3. Its eighteen handles worked the first rack; reconnected to the column, NETZ now closes the column's supply (only with the plant-room master closed), NOT-AUS opens it, SPÜLEN opens and shuts the column's outlet to the pump and RÜCKS. fits a fresh core, while the rest still await new duties. Its alarm windows flash until acknowledged with QUITT, and its channel grid, meters, IST and SOLL pointers, trend screen and red readouts repeat the column's state under their old 1986 names. The column takes its xenon from the plant room's cylinder bank through the cage regulator. The plate reading AUSSER BETRIEB has not been removed. Neither the wall map nor its pin lights is a substitute for local measurement.

## Contributions from fellow VX Junkies

The east wall carries a compact three-bay auxiliary process board: mint-enamel 1974 controls, a channel overview and a beige 1986 instrument retrofit with a CRT and numeric readouts. Its paper strip chart records ferrocore angular velocity. The surviving board repeats the rack's operating controls and measurements. The emergency decoherence hotline is mounted outside in the corridor beside the lab entrance; the reference drawers stand in the south-east corner. An [inspection view](https://vx3-simulator.fly.dev/?view=control-panel) faces the process board directly.

Fellow VX Junkies are invited to contribute apparatus, operating notes, circuit diagrams, incident reports or corrections. Simply open a pull request with your proposed addition to the workstation record. Please distinguish observed community lore from details invented for the recreation so the next operator knows which readings have actually been taken.

## Construction record

The cabinet and laboratory are rendered with Three.js. The three framed advertisements were generated for this recreation; their art direction is recorded in the [poster record](doc/art/poster-prompts.md). Most apparatus sound effects are synthesised in code using Web Audio and [ZzFX](https://github.com/KilledByAPixel/ZzFX) (MIT). The BBC Micro uses a [two-second startup recording](src/audio/assets/README.md) supplied as a reference by the owner. The cassette player's Play and Stop keys use edited excerpts of Joseph Sardin's [Cassette Player: Play and Pause](https://bigsoundbank.com/cassette-player-play-and-pause-s0570.html); its Fast Forward key uses an edited excerpt of his [Cassette Player: Rewind and Stop](https://bigsoundbank.com/cassette-player-rewind-and-stop-s0566.html). Both recordings are published by BigSoundBank under CC0. The [audio asset record](src/audio/assets/README.md) documents the edits. The workstation also uses Vite (MIT), TypeScript (Apache-2.0), Vitest (MIT), ESLint (MIT) and Prettier (MIT).

The plant room has the same internal dimensions as the laboratory, its own working steel door and 300 mm masonry walls. A mercury arc rectifier glows blue-white and buzzes when the master switch is closed. The xenon bank stands behind a working cage; its thick manifold and rubber hose connect to a labelled wall port immediately beside the cage. Opening the regulator raises delivery pressure and then indicated flow; the purple XE FLUSS lamp flashes below 2 SLPM. Beside the bank, a Synclex xenon recirculator cools return gas from the neighbouring facility. Its local KREISLAUF switch starts the loop when plant power is available; the west bay's XE RÜCKL. window warns while the powered loop is stopped. A brass relief valve bears the old VX3 serial beneath it. The laboratory and plant-room lights begin off and have separate wall switches. The sink initially drips about once every ten seconds. Working the hot tap on and off reseats its washer; its quiet drip can only be heard inside the laboratory.

The SPINDLE and FIELD instruments and the wall load meter carry operating redlines. Prolonged running beyond them heats the Encabulator drive, H-B coils or betaform amplifier until smoke rises from the affected apparatus and an entry appears in the log. Reducing the load and letting the assembly cool clears the smoke; the ashtray's ordinary cigarette smoke is unrelated to these readings.

The BBC keyboard has sculpted keycaps, red function keys, a brown cursor cluster, lock lamps, a printed deck strip and rear sockets. Clicking the computer clears the Cub, replays its boot sound and loads the diagnostic disk; the drive lamp follows disk activity. This restarts the computer without resetting the apparatus. Ten original German pages advise on master power, rack power, xenon supply, heater warm-up, drive and governor settings, and excessive speed. When the powered spindle turns with sufficient xenon, the program reports FERROKERNE DREHEN SICH. The [screen record](doc/art/bbc-monitor/README.md) includes the pre-rendered pages.
