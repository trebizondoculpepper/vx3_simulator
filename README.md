# VX3 Simulator

A workstation recreation of the VX3 laboratory that is still in service two floors below the Helmholtz-Bau at the University of Leipzig, Room −2.17. The **VX3** stands in the bay on the north wall: a ferrocore column, its vessel and its lifting head, on an olive skid between two mismatched cabinets of 19-inch units, fed from the adjacent TECHNIKRAUM. The apparatus is original; the ducting, cable trays and safety fittings are modern additions. The object of a run is to seal and purge the vessel, bring the valve heaters to temperature, establish spindle rotation, raise the field and hold a useful Delta without letting the field run off its balance. For educational use only.

The original equipment uses analogue regulation, valves, ferrocores and a Baltovsky chamber monitor. Its numerical counter and oscilloscope are observation instruments, not a computer control register. The later ConVX practice of driving a VX3 from a microcomputer is outside this recreation. No credible calculation of a VX8 on a conventional computer is presently available.

## Commissioning the workstation

Use Node.js 22.13.x, 24.x or 26+ and npm. From this directory:

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local address printed by the workstation, normally `http://127.0.0.1:5173/`. The full cold-start procedure, step by step with photographs, is the [cold-start runbook](doc/runbooks/cold_start.md); a short version is filed in the reference drawers. In outline: in the TECHNIKRAUM, close the HAUPTSCHALTER (the mercury arc rectifier feeds the machine's direct-current equipment; the lights, the east board and the xenon recirculator are on the mains), switch the recirculator on and open the xenon regulator to at least 2 SLPM. Switch the machine's supply on at the isolator on the west wall and in the base of each cabinet (A11 and B13). At B11 SÄULE, lower and clamp the head, open the xenon inlet and the beam-tube outlet, purge until O2 = 0 lights, and shut the outlet to let the pressure build past 1.2 bar. Close NETZ on the east board: the valve heaters, interlocked until the vessel is sealed, purged, pressurised and recirculating, warm and bring the core to a glow. Bring the drive up with DREHZAHL and FELD at B6 ANTRIEB and trim with TRIMM against the east board's IST and SOLL pointers; the drive's activity takes the core to full power, when it turns blue and spins. Heating the core in an unsealed or unpurged chamber burns it out; a core starved of xenon suffers a flux inductance catastrophe.

The run's “Blatz” trim scale and phase spindle are particular to this installation; they should not be mistaken for general VX nomenclature. A fully stable field still respects Yalgeth's 0.88 Delta limit.

Secondary controls shape the run. UNTERSETZUNG (B12 KERN) sets the gearing between the drive and the phase spindle and moves the trim point with it, so retrim after any change. ERDE? on the east board earths the plasma inducer, and the trim point then drifts far more slowly. The insulation band, on the key-locked SCHWEBUNG selector (B9), clears strain fastest when it matches the field strength.

Click the left half of any knob or rotary selector to turn it anticlockwise and the right half to turn it clockwise, one detent at a time; every one stops at its end stops rather than wrapping round. The emergency telephone's dial is not a knob and does not follow this rule. Knob markers rotate with their bodies about the shafts. Every knob and rotary selector has an independent setting; the plasma EXCITE and NULL controls are separate from coil FIELD and BLATZ TRIM, and each detector and amplifier has its own gain. In movement mode, aim at the appropriate half and click or press E; Escape releases the pointer. C crouches and V lowers the crouch further. Scroll to zoom in and out, up to four times closer, for working small controls; looking slows in proportion as the view narrows. A Magic Mouse swipe, the Arc Mouse touch strip and a trackpad pinch all zoom smoothly. Hold Option/Alt while scrolling to move the camera up or down, and a clearly sideways swipe steps sideways. Dragging the scene changes the viewing angle. The on-screen arrows move the operator and the plus and minus buttons zoom. The developer room at `/?room=dev0` walks and zooms the same way. The operator's log records adjustments and incidents; it may be put away and reopened. Sound begins after the first operator action and may be muted or adjusted at the top left.

## Represented equipment

The cabinets are numbered unit by unit from the top, on tags on their left uprights: cabinet A, the grey-blue rack on the left, A1 to A11, and cabinet B, the tall olive cabinet on the right, B1 to B13. Each has its supply in its base. Neumann Dynamik, Volt Xoccula, Gorskiy and others appear on the fascias. The precise maker-to-unit pairings, panels and wiring shown here are the recreation's own designs. Borrowed community terms and invented local parts are distinguished in the [lore register](doc/lore/summary.md) and [component catalogue](doc/lore/component-catalogue.json). How switches, knobs and levers reach the machine, and which lamps, needles and screens report back, is drawn in the [control graph](doc/design/control-graph.md).

The surrounding laboratory includes an analogue wall clock, a map of global flux correlations, a test bench with a BBC Micro Model B beneath its Microvitec Cub monitor and a separate 5¼-inch disk drive, with an orange typist's chair pulled up to it (sit in it and the Cub is square in front of you; C stands up again), and a safety notice board above the trunking on the west wall. A small amber-glass ashtray sits on the west workbench beside the deep white porcelain sink, its last cigarette still burning. Below the map, a wall meter shows instantaneous load in kW and accumulated consumption in kWh, and responds to the connected apparatus in this recreation. Three period advertisements just above the west workbench offer differing accounts of VX equipment and its likely operators. Three reference drawers hold the operating guide, core-handling instructions and a redrawn signal path. A Neumann Dynamik teleprinter stands on a bracket fixed to the right-hand wall, with continuous-feed stock below and old output gathering on the floor; just above it, a prismatic Time Dilation crystal glows and sheds particles once the ferrocores are warm, powered, sufficiently supplied with xenon and settled at the selected spindle speed. The printer records only operator actions that change the apparatus model, on timestamped continuous fanfold paper after a slightly varying one-to-two-second response; room fittings and automatic status reports stay in the notebook, with five quiet recordings of a real dot-matrix printer, varied for each printed line. Its supply never visibly runs out; printed paper drops into a perpetually settling pile whose height stays roughly constant. It keeps logging while machinery power is off, on its separate supply. On the east wall stands a 1974 process board, rewired in 1986 as a remote station for the VX3. Its eighteen handles work the drive: NETZ closes its breaker (only with the plant-room master closed and the machine switched on), NOT-AUS opens it, SPÜLEN opens and shuts the vessel's outlet to the pump, RÜCKS. fits a fresh core, and the rest switch the drive's circuits. The row of eighteen alarm windows across its top shows every warning in the installation, flashing until acknowledged with QUITT; its channel grid, meters, IST and SOLL pointers, green geometric CRT and red readouts repeat the drive's state. The machine takes its xenon from the plant room's cylinder bank through the cage regulator and the recirculator. The plate reading AUSSER BETRIEB has not been removed. Neither the wall map nor its pin lights is a substitute for local measurement.

## Contributions from fellow VX Junkies

The east wall carries a compact three-bay auxiliary process board: mint-enamel 1974 controls, a channel overview and a beige 1986 instrument retrofit with a CRT and numeric readouts. Its paper strip chart records ferrocore angular velocity. The board repeats the drive's operating controls and measurements. The emergency decoherence hotline is mounted outside in the corridor beside the lab entrance; the reference drawers stand in the south-east corner. An [inspection view](https://vx3-simulator.fly.dev/?view=control-panel) faces the process board directly.

Fellow VX Junkies are invited to contribute apparatus, operating notes, circuit diagrams, incident reports or corrections. Simply open a pull request with your proposed addition to the workstation record. Please distinguish observed community lore from details invented for the recreation so the next operator knows which readings have actually been taken.

## Construction record

The cabinet and laboratory are rendered with Three.js. The three framed advertisements were generated for this recreation; their art direction is recorded in the [poster record](doc/art/poster-prompts.md). Apparatus audio combines original Web Audio/ZzFX patches with edited recordings of a real dot-matrix printer, metal-door hinges, cassette keys and a BBC startup chime. Authors, source links, licences and edit notes are listed in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md); encoding details are kept in the [audio asset record](src/audio/assets/README.md). The workstation also uses Vite (MIT), TypeScript (Apache-2.0), Vitest (MIT), ESLint (MIT) and Prettier (MIT).

The plant room has the same internal dimensions as the laboratory, its own working steel door and 300 mm masonry walls. A mercury arc rectifier glows blue-white and buzzes when the master switch is closed. The xenon bank stands behind a working cage; its thick manifold and rubber hose connect to a labelled wall port immediately beside the cage. Opening the regulator raises delivery pressure and then indicated flow; the purple XE FLUSS lamp flashes below 2 SLPM. Beside the bank, a Synclex xenon recirculator cools return gas from the neighbouring facility. Its local KREISLAUF switch starts the loop when plant power is available; the west bay's XE RÜCKL. window warns while the powered loop is stopped. A brass relief valve bears the old VX3 serial beneath it. The laboratory and plant-room lights begin off and have separate wall switches. The sink initially drips about once every ten seconds. Working the hot tap on and off reseats its washer; its quiet drip can only be heard inside the laboratory.

Across the main corridor, the VX-Museum occupies a nine-metre gallery. Its door opens into the same walkable world: the operator can cross the threshold without a room change or reload. One corridor diagram was taken down to make its entrance. The gallery has its own lighting layer and switch, so its spotlights work independently of the corridor and laboratory. Eight plain plinths stand beneath individually aimed spotlights; three hold the resonator, copper solenoid and coil cabinet formerly kept on the laboratory workbench, while five await further exhibits. The museum's wall switch starts the spotlights. Framed portraits of VX luminaries, enlarged German editions of VX-Welt, VX Heute and Dein VX, and a Leipzig display record the DDR's place in the workstation's history. The north, east and west gallery doors remain locked pending future collections.

The SPINDLE and FIELD instruments and the wall load meter carry operating redlines. Prolonged running beyond them heats the Encabulator drive, H-B coils or betaform amplifier until smoke rises from the affected apparatus and an entry appears in the log. Reducing the load and letting the assembly cool clears the smoke; the ashtray's ordinary cigarette smoke is unrelated to these readings.

The BBC keyboard has sculpted keycaps, red function keys, a brown cursor cluster, lock lamps, a printed deck strip and rear sockets. Clicking the computer clears the Cub, replays its boot sound and loads the diagnostic disk; the drive lamp follows disk activity. This restarts the computer without resetting the apparatus. Ten original German pages advise on master power, rack power, xenon supply, heater warm-up, drive and governor settings, and excessive speed. When the powered spindle turns with sufficient xenon, the program reports FERROKERNE DREHEN SICH. The [screen record](doc/art/bbc-monitor/README.md) includes the pre-rendered pages.

The workbench cassette holds four original electronic tracks: Morgenstrom, Fernverbindung, Leichte Arbeit and Offener Horizont. Play resumes the tape, Stop holds its position, and Fast Forward advances audibly at five times normal speed and one-quarter volume, with the higher pitch of accelerated tape. Tracks run in order and the cassette repeats. Playback follows the workstation volume, mute setting and acoustic separation between rooms. The recordings and their generation history are acknowledged in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md).

The southern MESSWERTE bay projects the ferrocore state in green phosphor: a plane below 800 K, a cube from 800 K, a regular polyhedron from 2,000 K, a fractured polyhedron at the 3,200 K critical point and a four-dimensional hypercube projection above 3,700 K. A failed core leaves a STÖRUNG screen. These are precomputed projections, selected by the measured core temperature.
