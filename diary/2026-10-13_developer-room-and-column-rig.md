## Problem Statement

The user asked for a "developer room" in which to design and test new components before they join the game world, and for a design concept of a more interesting VX3 rack after reference art posted to r/VXJunkies: two mismatched racks on one skid with a vertically mounted column between them. The concept should be unwired but reuse the existing buttons and screens where possible, add 1980s-style push buttons, and use much smaller lettering for compact panels. During the work the user added: model each control, indicator and display individually; put placeholder text on the labels; make every control fully modelled and animated even though nothing is wired; and let 1980s push buttons carry an LED indicator whose colour the control specifies.

## Our Approach

The user first asked for a doorway in place of a poster. The lab's posters hang above the west workbench and back onto the plant room, so a doorway there would have needed the bench and the plant room moved. The user then said a URL would do, so the developer room is a separate page at `/?room=dev`, loaded only when asked for. Reddit blocks both the web fetcher and the browser extension, so the user pasted the reference image. It shows a grey-blue 19-inch rack (LCD controller with keypad, two big finned heatsinks, an open bay where a beam tube enters), an olive cabinet (twin controllers, amber LED readouts, a round gauge, meters, blank panels), and between them a vacuum column: a crown of black actuators on a top plate, flange stacks, a lifting frame with dark disc stages, and a ported chamber with viewports and a gate valve. The column became the vertically mounted ferrocore.

## Implementation

- `src/main.ts`: `?room=dev` dynamically imports `src/rendering/dev-room.ts`; the game is unchanged otherwise. The dev room builds as its own chunk.
- `src/rendering/dev-room.ts`: a plain lit room with a metre grid, orbit controls (`three/addons`, part of the existing `three` dependency), click to work any control, and P to switch between the game's PS1 resolution and full resolution for reading small print. `window.vx3Dev.view()` and `setPs1()` position the camera for scripted screenshots.
- `src/rendering/dev-parts-board.ts`: a catalogue board with one of every new part at four times life size, captioned, working and running.
- `src/scene/eighties-controls.ts`: small printed labels (`createMicroLabel`, a few millimetres high, drawn at a fixed pixel size per line), 19 mm push buttons (illuminated caps, or a plain cap with an LED window via `led: CapColour`; latching or momentary), 5 mm panel LEDs, seven-segment readouts and LED bar graphs.
- `src/scene/panel-switchgear.ts`: collet, skirted, instrument and pointer knobs with optional printed scales; toggles, illuminated rockers, key switches and slide switches; calculator-style keycaps and keypads; BNC and 4 mm sockets; finned heatsinks. Every moving part eases to its new position.
- `src/scene/panel-instruments.ts`: a moving-coil panel meter, a round gauge in a chrome bezel, a character LCD, a VFD, an amber LED matrix and jewel lamps. Needles ease toward their readings.
- `src/scene/ferrocore-column-rig.ts`: the concept, at real size in metres, laid out panel by panel after the reference. Printed labels show `LABEL nn` placeholders; the intended German legend is kept in the code and in each label mesh's name. The NETZ button on the bottom-left supply switches the whole concept on and off. The core glows through the column's windows and casts an orange point light onto the racks.

- Follow-up request (with a photo of a rotary isolator): two heavier parts in `panel-switchgear.ts`. `createKeyLockedKnob` is a bar knob with a lock barrel in its hub; a click on the hub turns the key, and the knob steps between its positions only while unlocked, otherwise giving a few degrees and springing back. The barrel belongs to the key's group, because the key alone is edge-on from the front and too thin to hit. `createPowerIsolator` is the surface-mounted IP65 isolator from the photo: grey box on a black back, yellow plate and ring, red handle that swings a quarter turn from OFF (across) to ON (upright), and a window showing the state. On the rig the selector replaces the plain pointer selector on the beat-frequency panel, and the isolator is mounted on the olive cabinet's right side as the rig's supply: OFF darkens everything whatever the NETZ button says. Both stand on a second catalogue board in the developer room, which now logs to the console which part each click worked.

- Follow-up: the column's head now rises and lowers. The two rigid struts and the drive rods are gone; everything above the chamber (mating flange, collar, bellows, top plate, crown, the core and its stage discs) is one group that two hydraulic rams behind the column carry through 0.38 m. The barrels stand on brackets off the chamber; the chrome rods and the yoke joining them ride with the head. Lowered, the core sits inside the chamber and four toggle clamps on the chamber's top flange swing over the mating flange. The user then explained how the game is meant to work: the machine has no purpose, and the game is working out its preposterous cold start. Purging does not light the core; the core's activation temperature (kelvin) and brightness are set by the game's internal model, not by knobs, and heating it in air burns it out. `src/game/column-prep.ts` models that cold start with tests: the rams, the clamps (which only close on a fully lowered head and then hold it), the chamber gas as partial pressures with an inlet through a regulator and an outlet to the pump, the bottle, the core's temperature following its set point with a lag, and two failures, burnout (hot in air or unsealed) and flux inductance catastrophe (a hot core with the outlet left open until the bottle runs dry). The column station on the olive cabinet works the rams, clamps and inlet; the beam tube's handwheel is the outlet; the top strip's readouts show core kelvin, chamber pressure and bottle contents. The core is dark grey when cold, glows from deep orange through white-hot to blue as it nears full power, and at full power pulses and spins. Sealed in the chamber, its light shows at every window as an animated view of coil bands sweeping past at its spin rate. In the developer room, sliders in a "game model stand-in" panel set the activation temperature and brightness, with a status line and the column's log beside them; `vx3Dev.work(name)` works a control by name for scripted tests.
- Follow-up: the leads from the column ran straight through the cabinets' side panels. The crown cables, the hydraulic hoses and the thermocouple lead now run back behind the column and round the backs of the cabinets to glands in their back panels; the crown cables are rebuilt as the head moves.

- Follow-up: a game-model test of the whole cold start. `src/game/cold-start.ts` composes the containment vessel (`column-prep.ts`), the catalogue's xenon supply components (run by `rig-model.ts`, as the plant room's bank is), the rectifier's HAUPTSCHALTER and the recirculator's KREISLAUF switch, the ferrocore drive (`rig.ts`) and the anomalies (`ferrocore-anomaly.ts`). The flow gauge reads the purge stream while purging and the recirculator's loop afterwards, so the drive's existing 2 SLPM check needs the recirculator; the core's temperature comes from the valve heaters and the cores' activity; a ruined core trips the breaker. `cold-start.test.ts` walks the user's procedure (lower and clamp, purge open, xenon on, plant power and recirculator, oxygen to zero, purge shut, mains, heaters, spindle and field, trim to lock) to the fully operational state and checks its effects, and reaches four failure states: burnout (lit before purging, or unclamped hot), flux inductance catastrophe (purge left open on a running core), and the existing breaker trips from field strain and from a dry circuit. A perfect run takes about a minute. `doc/design/cold-start.md` maps the procedure, the essential inputs, the model values worth a gauge and the failure hooks, noting which already exist in the scene; the one missing gauge that matters is an oxygen meter.

- Follow-up: the oxygen sensor and the purge port. The sensor is a narrowband lambda probe, like a motorcycle exhaust's, screwed into a boss on the chamber wall with its boot and lead routed to the olive cabinet's back. `column-prep.ts` now owns `oxygenPercent` and `lambdaVolts` (about 0.1 V in air, switching through 0.45 V near 0.3 % oxygen, about 0.85 V when it is gone); the column station's purity bar became a 0–1 V panel meter with a green band above 0.7 V and an O2 = 0 lamp, and the cold start test checks the probe as the purge proxy. Panel meters and round gauges gained a full-scale value and the meter a coloured band; both chamber pressure gauges now read 0–2.5 bar with red from the 1.8 bar regulator (they had read pressure on a 0–5 scale). The purge port is a stub and brass nozzle under the far end of the beam tube, inside the grey rack's bay just above its shelf. `src/scene/gas-vent.ts` draws heavy gas: a jet out of the nozzle that sinks and spreads across whatever it lands on, as xenon would; it runs while the purge is open on a sealed, pressurised chamber.

- Follow-up: a layout audit for the concept rig's panels. Every part fitted to a panel (displays, meters, gauges, controls, lamps, connectors and printed labels) is recorded with its panel, and `layoutProblems()` measures each part's footprint and reports any two closer than 1 mm (`PART_CLEARANCE`) or any within 5 mm of the panel edge (`EDGE_CLEARANCE`). Rack screws, handles, dividers and the windows behind readouts are mounting hardware, not fitted parts. The developer room runs it on load and warns in the console; `vx3Dev.layout()` returns the list. It checks 236 parts on 24 panels; with deliberately strict clearances (10 mm and 30 mm) it flags 141, so it sees them all. At the real limits it found four problems, the two evaporator controllers' title labels 4.5 mm from the top edge; their dark title strips, and the column controller's grey one, ran edge to edge too. All three strips are now inset 5 mm from every edge with their labels on them, and the audit reports none.

- Follow-up: a first prototype of the core's sound, in plain Web Audio as the sound brief requires (no new packages). The core's spin moved into the vessel model (`spinRate`, `spinAngle`) so picture and sound share it. `src/audio/core-voice-parameters.ts` (pure, tested) maps temperature, brightness, spin and sealing to synth settings: silent when cold, fading in over the last 150 K before activation; an FM drone with a non-integer modulator ratio climbing from 35 Hz at 800 K to 110 Hz at 3,200 K, brightening as its FM depth and filter rise; a ring-modulated shimmer near full power; a once-per-revolution level and filter sweep; a tick as each of the four tie bars passes; a pulse at the core's visible pulse rate at full power; muffled when sealed in the chamber. `src/audio/core-voice.ts` is the patch, with a collapsing pitch sweep for the flux inductance catastrophe and a crackle for burnout. The developer room has a Sound button and drives the voice from the model each frame; `core-voice-render.ts` renders set scenes offline to WAV for listening (`vx3Dev.renderVoice`). Spectrograms of the renders showed every clip starting with a swoop down from the oscillators' default 440 Hz; the first update now sets values outright.
- Follow-up: equipment tags with real wording (the panels keep their placeholders): O2-SONDE / LAMBDA on the chamber wall below the probe, a brass SPÜLVENTIL / XE-KREIS tag hung from the purge valve's stem, SPÜLGAS-AUSLASS on the beam tube above the purge port (replacing a placeholder Dymo strip), and XE-EINLASS on the chamber stand where the feed comes in. Two first placements were partly hidden, one by a flange bolt and one by the thermocouple lead, and were moved.

## Outcomes

- All checks pass (146 tests); the main game still loads; clicks were verified headlessly on the rocker, key switch and slide switch.
- A first pass looked slimmer than the reference. Measuring the photo showed the column was the right size but the racks were 1.2 m apart instead of about 0.65 m, so the skid was cut to about 2 m and the racks pulled in; a heavier collar, a wider crown, more cables and the core light added weight.
- Bugs found and fixed from screenshots: seven-segment digits came apart when each segment was slanted separately (now one skew for the whole canvas); readouts were hidden behind black inset blocks drawn in front of them; the round gauge's square face showed past its bezel; only the key, not the barrel, of a key switch took clicks, so whole parts are now the click targets.
